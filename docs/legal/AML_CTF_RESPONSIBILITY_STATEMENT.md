This statement exists to draw a clear line between what the Platform's AML-related feature actually
does, and the Brokerage's own anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorism-financing obligations,
which the Platform does not replace, discharge, automate, or take on. It is not legal advice on the
Brokerage's AML/CTF obligations, and it does not certify that either the Platform or the Brokerage
meets any AML/CTF requirement.

## 1. What the Platform's AML feature actually is

The Platform provides a workflow and record-keeping feature that lets Brokerage staff track the
status of a party's AML/identity verification against a property transaction (for example, "Not
Started," "In Progress," "Complete," "Review Required"), record supporting evidence references, and
manage exceptions that need follow-up. **During this pilot, the AML integration operates in
sandbox/test mode only** (see the Subprocessor and Third-Party Service Disclosure document) — no
real identity-verification check is performed by, or through, the Platform, and no status shown by
the Platform during the pilot reflects a real completed check unless and until a live AML vendor is
separately configured and disclosed.

## 2. What the Platform does not do

The Platform does not: perform a real identity or source-of-funds check; make a determination of
AML/CTF risk on the Brokerage's behalf; lodge a suspicious matter report, threshold transaction
report, or any other statutory report with AUSTRAC or any other regulator; maintain the Brokerage's
AML/CTF program; or otherwise perform any statutory audit or regulatory compliance function. The
Platform's role is limited to helping the Brokerage keep track of its own process — it is a tool,
not a compliance officer.

## 3. Whose obligation this is

The Brokerage remains solely responsible for meeting whatever AML/CTF obligations apply, or come to
apply, to its business, including under the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing
Act 2006* (Cth) and any associated rules. **Whether, and from when, real estate agents in Australia
are treated as reporting entities under that Act (including under any "Tranche 2" reform extending
AML/CTF obligations to real estate) is a live legislative and regulatory question that this draft
does not resolve — a qualified Australian lawyer must confirm the Brokerage's current obligations
as at the time this pilot actually runs**, and this Statement must be updated to match whatever is
confirmed, rather than assuming either that such obligations do or do not currently apply.

Using the Platform's AML tracking feature does not create, satisfy, or transfer any part of that
obligation. If the Brokerage has an AML/CTF obligation, it must meet it through its own program and
processes, using the Platform (if at all) only as a record-keeping aid for its own internal
purposes.

## 4. No trust money

Separately from AML/CTF, the Platform does not hold, receive, or transmit trust money at any point.
Any funds — trust money or otherwise — relevant to a transaction the Brokerage manages using the
Platform remain entirely within the Brokerage's own bank/trust account arrangements, governed by the
Brokerage's obligations under Queensland property agent trust account legislation (for example, the
*Agents Financial Administration Act 2014* (Qld) — **reference to be confirmed as current** by a
qualified Australian lawyer).
